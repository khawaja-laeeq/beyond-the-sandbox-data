(R1) Central coding judgments (D2 authorization interpretation, D5 effect branches) were substantially assigned or revised by AI agents rather than the human coder (Appendix A, Table V), and the paper's only check on this — a 44/45 agreement test against the AI-produced codes — is explicitly called 'partly circular... not evidence of reliability'; the paper does not reconcile this with its own RQ2 critique of trusting elicited/self-reported model statements as weak evidence, leaving the reliability of its own key annotations as unaddressed as the model self-reports it criticizes.

The headline 0/45 therefore characterizes the retained cell definitions as well as disclosure structure.

(R1) The headline cross-case finding 'No cell has whole-proposition support from two organizations' (S2: 0/45) follows close to by construction from the proposition-size rule, which requires a single reference to state every element of a (often compound) proposition; with compound propositions and organizationally siloed reporting, near-total failure of 'whole support' is close to guaranteed regardless of underlying evidence quality, so the finding's apparent strength partly reflects the coding rule's strictness rather than an empirical discovery about disclosure practice (the paper partially acknowledges this but still foregrounds the 0/45 count in the abstract, intro, and conclusion as a central result).
