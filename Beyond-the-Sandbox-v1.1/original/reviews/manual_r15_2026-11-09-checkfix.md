VERDICT: FAIL
SCORES: overall 5.5 (R1 6, R2 5); bar 7; soundness 5 · novelty 4.5 · significance 5.5 · clarity 6 · reproducibility 7
REASONS:
1. Mean overall score 5.5 is below the acceptance bar of 7.0.
2. Soundness below 6 from R2 (4).
3. (R1) The headline claim that no cell has whole-proposition support from two organizations (S2: 0/45) rests almost entirely on a late, single-coder-authored 'proposition size' rule (Round 14, R14-1b); under the simpler 'single whole-supporting reference' variant the count is 39/6 with only one cell (Hugging Face D4) changing, showing the dramatic S2=0/45 figure is largely an artifact of how 'whole support' was defined rather than evidence of independently convergent organizational reporting.
4. (R2) The abstract and conclusion report six stopping mechanisms, but Table II includes 'Signal only' for OpenAI May, whose coded evidence explicitly states that no halt is reported. The evidence supports six response categories, not six mechanisms of stopping.
5. (R1) There is no independent second coding, and the majority of net status changes (7 of 8) plus all D5-branch and D2-annotation assignments were made by AI agents rather than the human coder; the paper's own 'AI re-check matched 44 of 45' statistic is explicitly acknowledged as partly circular (the re-checker re-verified codes it had itself helped produce), leaving no non-circular reliability evidence for the central 45-cell artifact.
6. (R2) The D5 persistent-state rule is applied inconsistently: repository creation establishes P for OpenAI May, while the AISI Mythos 5 coding excludes P because the contribution was refused despite recording a posted malicious pull request. Refusal of incorporation does not establish absence of persistent platform state, and the codebook supplies no decision rule distinguishing these artifacts.
7. (R1) Novelty is explicitly disclaimed ('we claim no new comparison, grading method, distinction or taxonomy') and the automated novelty check found strong overlap with four cited works (Raftari, Bouke, Amundsen, Bieringer) that already cover the same three incident clusters and near-identical dimensions (access/belief/action/response/harm); the claimed increment is confined to a specific presentation format (per-cell deciding passages, organization counts, rule recounts) rather than a new empirical or methodological result.
8. (R2) The headline zero-cell two-organization result depends on proposition aggregation. Hugging Face D4 combines each organization's response into one proposition, making each organization's account partial by construction; the sensitivity analysis does not test splitting this compound proposition into separately checkable claims.
9. (R1) The study is built almost entirely on first-party disclosures issued by the very organizations whose own detection, containment, and authorization conduct is being evaluated (OpenAI for OpenAI incidents, Anthropic for Anthropic incidents, AISI for AISI incidents), yet nowhere discusses the risk that such self-reports may be shaped by reputational or legal incentives to minimize fault — a substantive gap given RQ4 is specifically about what claims these disclosures can support.
10. (R2) Methods reports no independent human assessment of the final passage-entailment decisions, D2 annotations or D5 branches. The quotation and manuscript-data checks establish textual presence and consistency, while the partly circular AI re-check supplies no independent evidence of coding reliability.
11. (R1) The coding underwent at least 14 documented rounds of revision on a fixed evidentiary base, including two cells that were changed and then restored to their original value (Hugging Face D1, D2); this repeated back-and-forth recoding by a single coder plus AI agents on unchanged source material raises a garden-of-forking-paths concern that coding rules were adjusted toward particular conclusions, which the sensitivity analysis (S0–S6) only partially mitigates since it does not independently re-derive the proposition-size rule that drives the paper's main finding.
12. (R2) RQ2 asks about both reality recognition and authorization interpretation, but only authorization interpretation is systematically coded. The descriptive treatment of reality recognition cannot support a complete cross-unit comparison of the relationship between those two constructs.
