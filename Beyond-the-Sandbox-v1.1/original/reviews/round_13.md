SCORES: overall 4.5 (R1 5, R2 4); bar 7; soundness 4.5 · novelty 4.5 · significance 5.5 · clarity 5.5 · reproducibility 5.5
REASONS:
1. Mean overall score 4.5 is below the acceptance bar of 7.0.
2. Soundness below 6 from R1 (5), R2 (4).
3. (R1) The coding is single-coder with no independent human check. The Hugging Face D2 and D1 statuses were revised by AI agents and by lead decisions made through defaults. Seven of the 45 statuses were changed by AI agents, and all nine effect levels were assigned by AI. The same-document AI re-check (44/45) is not validation. The paper's headline cell-level findings therefore rest on an unvalidated instrument.
4. (R2) The S2 result of 1 documented/44 unknown decisions is not supported under the stated whole-proposition rule. Hugging Face D4 retains both organizations' detection/response and OpenAI containment, but the supplied R03 passage establishes only Hugging Face's detection and response. The evidence shown does not establish that each organization supports the whole retained proposition.
5. (R1) The headline findings are artifacts of rule choices and a single contested coding decision. 'D2 has the most unknowns' rests on how D2 is defined. The sole two-organization cell (Hugging Face D4) depends on a later reference addition and disappears under S6. The paper concedes that it cannot show the two accounts independently establish first detection. The result is a count of how the documents were coded, and it supports no substantive claim about disclosure.
6. (R2) Sections III.D and VI disclose no independent human assessment of the revised coding or effect levels. Seven net status changes and all nine effect levels were assigned by AI agents; the same-document consistency check does not establish coding reliability.
7. (R1) The central findings are close to tautological. Unknown statuses are concentrated in D2 because D2 is defined to require an explicit permission statement. 'Only one cell has two-organization whole-proposition support' follows from six of the nine units having documents from only one organization (Table VIII: 'cannot meet S2 by construction'). No inferential content remains beyond the construction.
8. (R2) The D2 decision boundary is insufficiently operationalized across cases. Anthropic internal-model D2 accepts reported reasoning about other CTF competitors' targets, while Anthropic Mythos 5 D2 rejects reported reasoning treating scanners as exercise actors. Appendix A does not specify a decision rule that clearly distinguishes these closely related scope attributions.
9. (R1) Novelty is limited. The automated novelty check returned INCREMENTAL. Raftari, Bouke, Amundsen and Bieringer already cover incident comparison, claim grading, the reachability/authorization distinction and reporting fields. The authors disclaim novelty for each of these. The remaining increment is a per-cell artifact and recounts, which is a modest contribution for a venue audience.
10. (R2) The reproducibility claim exceeds the materials presented: the manuscript names released supplementary files but supplies no artifact location, executable recount procedure, dependency settings or complete selection frame. The supplied appendix_b_check.csv verifies reference equality, whereas Appendix E claims that this check also verifies statuses, effect levels and branch letters.
11. (R1) The effect-level (L1/L2/L3) scale is ad hoc and unvalidated, and the paper admits it is threshold-dependent. It was assigned by AI agents, and the split is 0/1/8 under S0, 0/2/7 under S3, 0/3/6 under S3b and 0/4/5 under S3c. Eight of nine units are L3 at baseline, so the scale gives almost no discrimination.
12. (R2) The contribution is narrow relative to the documented prior-work overlap in Section II and Appendix D. The principal surviving finding concerns missing authorization documentation in a purposively fixed corpus; no new security mechanism, validated measurement instrument or defense result is established.
