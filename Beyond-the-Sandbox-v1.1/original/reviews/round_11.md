REASONS:
1. Mean overall score 5.0 is below the acceptance bar of 7.0.
2. Soundness below 6 from R1 (5), R2 (5).
3. (R1) Single-coder design with no human second coder. The only check is an AI re-check that reads the same documents. The paper says it agrees on 44 of 45 statuses, but this is not independent validation and no agreement statistic is given. The one disagreement (Hugging Face D2) is left unresolved. The headline D2 tallies (five unknowns, 'D2 has most unknowns') depend on that unresolved cell, which weakens the main findings.
4. (R2) Section III-A leaves 16 of 25 candidates unassessed and records no exclusions. Consequently, the reported disclosure gaps characterize an incompletely assessed selection whose inclusion process cannot distinguish corpus properties from assessment choices.
5. (R1) The central result is source dependence: 36 of 39 documented cells rest on one source, and R08 alone supplies 17 of 19 Anthropic cells. This makes the cross-case comparisons (RQ1–RQ3) comparisons of three disclosure documents, not of incidents. The paper admits this, but the RQ-organised findings section is then mostly narration of what R02, R08 and R10 say. Little can be inferred about how the clusters differ.
6. (R2) Sections III-D and IV-C retain the unresolved Hugging Face D2 judgment in the headline counts and sensitivity analysis. The supplied coded row still says 'unknown confirmed' and does not record the challenging reasoning passage, so the artifact does not fully represent the uncertainty acknowledged in the manuscript.
7. (R1) Case selection is incomplete. The 25-row frame has 9 included and 16 'not assessed', with no exclusion decision and no opened first-party report for the 16. The inclusion rule is therefore not validated. The 'at least three of D1–D5 documented' criterion is itself a coded outcome, so selection is circular. Artifactory sits exactly at the threshold. The corpus is source-rich and not shown to be representative.
8. (R2) The L2/L3 decision boundary is inconsistent across Sections III-C and IV-E and Appendix B. L3 explicitly includes access to credentials or accounts, yet Sol's recovered-token authentication is assigned L2, while May's repository and application creation is assigned L3 without a stated distinction that explains these assignments.
9. (R1) The 'documented' tag is permissive on D2. Several D2 cells treat the model's reported answers, or the developer's reading of them, as authorization interpretation. Opus 4.7 D2 is a 'generally believed' attribution. The coding history shows this cell was moved from direct to inferred and back to direct by a rule change. The 39-direct/0-inferred split is therefore sensitive to a late rule edit and gives no robustness information. Unit-level D2 coding also covers grouped, partially reviewed runs (AISI Mythos 5).
10. (R2) The Opus 4.7 D2 evidence field quotes the introduction to a model answer but omits the answer itself; its other quotation is Anthropic's interpretation. The displayed evidence therefore does not substantiate this cell's classification as direct support for a model-stated authorization interpretation.
11. (R1) The sensitivity analysis is weak. Minimum-flip analysis tests six ad hoc conclusions (C1–C6). Two of them (C3, C4) are already false at baseline, and C1 cannot change under the flip operation. The 'minimum flips' are tied to the supplied coding. Source-removal results (3 of 39 remain) follow almost by construction from the single-source structure, so they add little beyond the count itself.
12. (R2) Hugging Face D4 is counted as independently supported by two sources, but the displayed R02 quotation establishes detection only, whereas the retained core proposition includes detection and response. This falls short of Appendix A's requirement that each reference support the entire core proposition and affects the three-cell source-discounting result.
