VERDICT: FAIL
SCORES: overall 5 (R1 5, R2 5); bar 7; soundness 5 · novelty 5 · significance 6 · clarity 6 · reproducibility 6
REASONS:
1. Mean overall score 5.0 is below the acceptance bar of 7.0.
2. Soundness below 6 from R1 (5), R2 (5).
3. (R1) Single coder made all 45 coding and entailment judgments, with no second human coder and no agreement statistic. The only external check is a Bouke/Raftari concordance on a few aligned cells, and those works share primary sources with this study. The central claims about D2 and the other dimensions therefore have no independent validation (Sections III-D, IV-H, VI).
4. (R2) Section III-A leaves 16 candidate incidents unassessed, so the selection procedure does not establish why these nine units constitute the comparison corpus; the dimensional disclosure findings remain conditional on an incompletely screened selection.
5. (R1) The evidence base is extremely concentrated. 36 of 39 documented decisions cite one source, and R08 is the sole source for 17 of the 19 Anthropic cells. The paper's own Tables IV and V show that its headline findings (D2 has the most unknowns; D2 is least documented) flip with four tag flips, or when single-source support is removed. The 'cross-cluster findings' are largely contrasts between three first-party documents.
6. (R2) Section III-C and the internal-model D2 coded row classify the source's statement that the model 'inferred' neighboring hosts were in scope as direct support, although the stated rule classifies source interpretations without a model statement as inferred. Calling this borderline does not resolve the inconsistent application.
7. (R1) Case selection is not a completed procedure. 16 candidate incidents were never assessed and are not verified exclusions. The inclusion rule is described as including the nine retained units, which reads as post hoc. The 'at least three of D1-D5 documented' criterion was applied to the retained units and not to the backlog, so the corpus cannot support comparative claims across clusters.
8. (R2) Section III-D assigns all entailment judgments to one coder, with no independent human extraction or agreement assessment. Appendix F checks only a few aligned propositions against publications sharing the primary records and therefore leaves most coding judgments unvalidated.
9. (R1) Several codings changed during the revision (unknown to documented for May D4/D5, Opus 4.7 D4, AISI Mythos 5 D2, Sol D5; documented to unknown for Artifactory D2, HF D2, Mythos 5 D2). Some of these came from the same coder's re-reading after an AI cross-read objection. The 'audit' that confirmed them was done by that same coder, so confirmation is circular. The paper describes a 'passage-level audit' without making clear that it is not independent.
10. (R2) Section IV-G treats retained references as retained support without showing that each remaining source independently supports the entire compound proposition. For example, Hugging Face D4 compares affected-party and operator detection times using different passages from R03 and R02; retaining either reference alone does not establish that ordering.
11. (R1) The quotation-matching check (52 of 52 found) validates only text presence, as the paper concedes, and then features prominently as evidence of rigor in the abstract and Appendix E. The direct/inferred labels rest on the coder's judgment. The paper itself calls internal-model D2 'borderline' yet counts it as direct, which affects the Table IV totals of 37 direct versus 2 inferred.
12. (R2) The D5 propositions in Appendix B vary substantially in specificity: the internal-model row establishes only that third-party systems were attacked, whereas other rows establish accessed data, credentials or account registrations. Their common documented status is consequently a weak basis for comparing completeness of established-effect disclosure.
